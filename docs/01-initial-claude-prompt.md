Help me write a clause code prompt to implement the following idea.

Idea: a web-based interface for progressively digesting a long text.

Motivation: Pope Leo released Magnifica Humanitas on AI which I really want to read, but it’s 40k words (basically a small book), and I don’t have time right now to read it all. So either I skim it, or read a hot take, or put it in my “to read” list and maybe get around to it one way. I need a better way to approach it NOW, in a way that supports progressive engagement and learning. AI can help solve this problem!

Idea: the basic idea is that I need a system to progressively engage with a text or body of texts. In this example, the immediate text is MH, and the body of texts is a concentric circle of Catholic social teaching, the Catholic magisterium more generally, and everything it cites, etc. For MH I’m envisioning a page with some brief text at the top (title, what it is) then a selector button of “I want to engage with this in [1 min, 5 min, 20 min, 1 hr, 2 hr]”. Then is given only the actual text of the document, but only enough of it that you could reasonable get through in that time, with easy ways to click and dive in more to reveal more of the text. On the left is a floating TOC so you know where you are in the whole work. On the right is a way to open the references of the document in line to not lose the flow while reading. Maybe there are small AI-generated summaries of sections to give you context on the parts you’re skipping, but if so these are stylistically distinct from the main text since the goal is to focus on the main text itself.

Other:
To do this, you first need an automated way to scan a text and rank each “passage” in terms of the value it brings to understanding the whole or its subsection. That way you can generate a list of what passages are included in each time segment of engagement.

You need natural ways to dive deeper without getting distracted from the main text:
- “…” for skipped portions of the text that open the next layer when you click on them
- footnotes with additional context opened to the side to not distract from the main flow

MH is a particular use case, but this idea of progressive engagement can be applied to any text or body of texts. You could use it to give more deeply into the history of Catholic social thought, which is the subject of one of the first chapters of MH.

Maybe this is a website you can stand up for processing any text.

Or maybe this is a tool you use in the form of an obsidian plugin or something where you are able to more directly integrate it with markdown files so that you can take notes and build a knowledge base and clip from the text as you read.
